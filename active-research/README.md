# Active Research Track

The active research track is for students who have completed the AIEA Lab onboarding tasks and are ready to conduct independent research. Instead of following weekly onboarding assignments, active researchers define and carry out a project in coordination with a project lead.

## Before the quarter begins

- [ ] Confirm your project and research lead.
- [ ] Agree on the research question or problem area you will investigate.
- [ ] Join the project’s communication channels, meetings, repositories, and shared documents.
- [ ] Confirm how you will receive course or independent-study credit, if applicable.
- [ ] Review the project README, reading list, existing publications, and current codebase.

## Research plan

During the first two weeks, create a short research plan with your lead. It should include:

- the research question and why it matters;
- a falsifiable hypothesis or clearly defined engineering objective;
- relevant prior work and the baseline you will use;
- the proposed method, data, and evaluation metrics;
- expected compute, software, data, and collaboration needs;
- ethical, privacy, safety, or reproducibility considerations;
- milestones for the quarter and a fallback plan if the original approach fails;
- the intended final artifacts, such as code, a dataset, experimental results, or a paper draft.

The plan is a working document. Update it when results or constraints change, and discuss substantial changes with your lead.

## Weekly expectations

- Attend the project’s working sessions and other meetings agreed upon with your lead.
- Maintain a written record of experiments, configurations, results, failures, and decisions.
- Push work regularly to the appropriate repository using focused commits and descriptive messages.
- Keep experiments reproducible by recording code versions, environments, data splits, configurations, and random seeds.
- Share a concise weekly update covering progress, evidence, blockers, and next steps.
- Ask for help early when access, compute, data, or research decisions block progress.
- Delete idle Nautilus deployments and follow the project’s data and credential-handling requirements.

## Suggested quarter milestones

### Weeks 1–2: Scope

Finalize the research plan, establish the baseline, and verify that the required code and data are accessible.

### Weeks 3–4: Baseline

Reproduce or validate the baseline and document discrepancies before introducing major changes.

### Weeks 5–7: Investigate

Implement the proposed method, run controlled experiments, and revise the plan based on evidence.

### Weeks 8–9: Evaluate

Complete the primary evaluation, analyze failures, run essential ablations, and identify threats to validity.

### Week 10: Communicate and hand off

Finalize the report, presentation, code, and documentation so another project member can reproduce and continue the work.

## End-of-quarter deliverables

Unless your lead or syllabus specifies a different format, complete the following:

- [ ] A technical report that explains the question, related work, method, experimental setup, results, limitations, and next steps.
- [ ] A presentation or project demonstration for the lab.
- [ ] A clean repository or branch containing code, configuration, and reproduction instructions.
- [ ] Links to the commits, datasets, experiment logs, and other artifacts used in the report.
- [ ] A short handoff note describing the current state, unresolved problems, and recommended next experiment.
- [ ] Any Canvas or independent-study submission required for course credit.

Do not include secrets, restricted data, or large generated artifacts in Git. Store them in the project-approved location and document how authorized collaborators can access them.

## Getting help

Your project lead is the first contact for research scope and project-specific decisions. Use the appropriate lab channel for infrastructure or access questions. If you need to change projects, pause an experiment with significant cost, or revise the quarter’s main objective, discuss it with your lead before proceeding.
